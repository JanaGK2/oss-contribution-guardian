# Philosophy

## The Core Idea

Open-source contribution is a social act as much as a technical one. You are entering a project maintained by people with limited time, specific goals, and established ways of working. The single most important thing you can do before contributing is read what the project itself says it expects — not what you assume, not what was true in the last project you worked on.

This skill is built around one principle: **the repository's own documentation takes precedence over everything else.**

---

## What This Skill Is

A structured guide that helps you read the right things in the right order, notice what is missing, and ask the right questions before you commit time (yours and the maintainer's) to a contribution that may not land.

It is opinionated about process. It is not opinionated about code style, language, framework, or tooling — those are the project's decisions, not ours.

---

## What This Skill Is Not

**It is not a legal advisor.** License compatibility, IP ownership, CLA obligations, and employer agreements have legal dimensions that require a qualified attorney. The skill can surface factual information about license types and quote what a CLA says. It cannot tell you what that means for your specific legal situation.

**It is not a security scanner.** Detecting secrets, vulnerable dependencies, or license violations in code requires dedicated tooling. This skill analyzes documentation.

**It is not a substitute for reading the docs yourself.** The skill helps you read faster and notice more. It is not meant to be a black box that says "you're clear to contribute." You remain responsible for your contribution.

---

## Why Maintainer Docs Win

General OSS guidance — including this skill — is necessarily generic. Maintainers know their project, community, and constraints in ways no general guide can anticipate. When `CONTRIBUTING.md` says something different from a general best practice, follow `CONTRIBUTING.md`. The maintainer wrote it because they have a reason.

If `CONTRIBUTING.md` conflicts with `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` or `SECURITY.md`, surface the conflict and recommend asking the maintainer. Don't resolve it by choosing a winner.

---

## Why "Responsible" Is in the Name

OSS contribution is often framed around career metrics: GitHub green squares, profile activity, resume lines. This skill takes a different frame.

Responsible contribution means:
- The maintainer gains something useful, not just review work
- Your change is well-understood before it is proposed
- You have thought through the implications (license, IP, security) before creating work for others
- You are prepared to be told no and accept it gracefully

That is the behavior this skill is designed to produce.

---

## What Counts as Sufficient Evidence

The skill will flag uncertainty rather than fill gaps with assumptions. A conclusion is only stated with confidence if it is directly supported by repo documentation. When evidence is partial, the response says so.

"I could not verify this from the available documentation" is a valid and useful output. Inventing a confident answer is not.
